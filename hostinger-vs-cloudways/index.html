Hostinger vs Cloudways: different products, not a photo finish

These two names show up in the same “hosting” searches, but they are not interchangeable. Hostinger is commonly shopped as a beginner / shared-style host (and also sells other families). Cloudways is a managed cloud layer on top of public cloud providers. Comparing them only on a 10-point score hides that fork.

Who this is for

You have heard both brands and want to know which job each one is built for. Typical readers: a student who outgrew a static site, or a small-site owner who was told to “just get Cloudways” without knowing that implies a cloud VM under the panel. If you are still choosing criteria for a first paid host, start with beginners or students.

Who this is not for

People hunting a single “fastest WordPress host 2026” winner. We have not measured speed. People who already have a cloud account and a configuration-management habit — you already know you are not shopping shared hosting.

What each one is (plain language)

Hostinger (official site: hostinger.com) is not one SKU. Their public pricing and product pages list several families — for example web hosting aimed at individuals and small sites, cloud-style plans, VPS, and other products such as business email. A panel-style account and a VM-shaped plan are not the same job. Before you compare Hostinger to Cloudways, confirm which Hostinger product you are looking at on hostinger.com/pricing and hostinger.com/web-hosting. We do not paste list prices into this page; they move.

Cloudways (official site: Cloudways) sells managed hosting on infrastructure from public clouds. On their pricing page (checked 2026-09-04) they present product shapes such as Flexible (pick a cloud provider and server size) and Autonomous (a more hands-off WordPress-oriented line). You pick a provider/size in their UI; they operate the stack (updates, a platform panel, add-ons). You are closer to “a VM with a management layer” than to “a shared account on a name you never see.” Read cloudways.com/en/pricing.php the same day you decide. Do not collapse their catalog into one number, and do not copy hourly or monthly figures from memory into a comparison.

When Hostinger is the more reasonable first look

  • You want a conventional beginner checkout and a documented app installer on a panel-style plan.
  • You are comparing other shared-style hosts such as SiteGround — see Hostinger vs SiteGround.
  • You do not want to choose a cloud datacenter size on day one.
  • You already confirmed you are shopping a Hostinger web hosting (or similar panel) product — not their VPS line by accident.

When Cloudways is the more reasonable first look

  • You already understand that a site runs on a VM, and you want someone to manage the stack.
  • You expect to resize compute later rather than jump between shared SKUs.
  • You were sent here because a tutorial mentioned DigitalOcean, AWS, Vultr, Linode/Akamai, or Google Cloud under a management panel — verify the current Cloudways provider list on their official pricing page the same day.

Decision close (not a winner)

If you cannot answer “panel account vs managed VM,” stop and read shared vs managed cloud first. If the site is still static, stop and read how to deploy a static site — paying for either brand to serve index.html is optional. We will not crown a winner.
