Hostinger vs Cloudways: different products, not a photo finish

These two names show up in the same “hosting” searches, but they are not interchangeable. Hostinger is commonly shopped as a beginner / shared-style host. Cloudways is a managed cloud layer on top of public cloud providers. Comparing them only on a 10-point score hides that fork.

Who this is for

You have heard both brands and want to know which job each one is built for. Typical readers: a student who outgrew a static site, or a small-site owner who was told to “just get Cloudways” without knowing that implies a cloud VM under the panel.

Who this is not for

People hunting a single “fastest WordPress host 2026” winner. We have not measured speed. People who already have a cloud account and a configuration-management habit — you already know you are not shopping shared hosting.

What each one is (plain language)

Hostinger (official site: hostinger.com) sells hosting plans aimed at individuals and small sites, including shared-style and other plan families. You typically get a control panel, app installers, and a beginner checkout. Read their current catalog rather than our memory of an old banner.

Cloudways (official site: cloudways.com) sells managed hosting on infrastructure from public clouds. You pick a provider/size in their UI; they operate the stack (updates, a platform panel, add-ons). You are closer to “a VM with a management layer” than to “a shared account on a name you never see.”

Do I want a shared-style beginner panel or a managed cloud VM? This is the actual product difference. If you cannot answer it, read shared vs managed cloud first.
Who is the infrastructure operator? On Cloudways you should know which public cloud sits underneath (their current list is on their site). On Hostinger you are buying Hostinger’s own hosting stack unless a specific product says otherwise.
What is billed — a boxed plan or cloud size + platform fee? Cloudways-style billing often tracks server size and add-ons. Shared-style plans are often a single SKU. We will not quote amounts. Check each provider’s current pricing page.
How do I get off later? Export files + database. Managed cloud may also mean snapshots on the underlying VM. Confirm in official docs before launch day.
Do I need git-based deploys or a file manager? Neither brand is “GitHub Pages.” If your site is static, you may not need either — see deploy a static site.

When Hostinger is the more reasonable first look

  • You want a conventional beginner checkout and a documented app installer.
  • You are comparing other shared-style hosts such as SiteGround — see Hostinger vs SiteGround.
  • You do not want to choose a cloud datacenter size on day one.

When Cloudways is the more reasonable first look

  • You already understand that a site runs on a VM, and you want someone to manage the stack.
  • You expect to resize compute later rather than jump between shared SKUs.
  • You were sent here because a tutorial mentioned DigitalOcean, AWS, Vultr, or similar under a management panel — verify the current Cloudways provider list on their official page.

FAQ

Is Cloudways “better” than Hostinger?
It is a different shape of product. Better for a VM workflow; usually more to learn for a first HTML assignment. We will not crown a winner.
Can I host a static student site on either?
You can put static files almost anywhere, but paying for a panel to serve index.html is optional. GitHub Pages is $0.
