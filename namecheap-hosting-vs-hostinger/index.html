Namecheap hosting vs Hostinger

People often meet Namecheap as a domain registrar and Hostinger as a hosting checkout. You can buy a domain in one place and host in another. This page is about whether Namecheap hosting and Hostinger hosting are even the same job — not about which logo is “#1 in 2026.”

Who this is for

You already have (or expect to have) a Namecheap account for domains, and a friend said “just add hosting there.” Or you are a student comparing a first paid host after a $0 static deploy.

Who this is not for

Anyone who only needs DNS for GitHub Pages. You can keep the domain at a registrar and skip hosting SKUs entirely. Anyone shopping managed cloud VMs — see Hostinger vs Cloudways.

Two products, sometimes one cart

Domains are a registration in a global namespace. Hosting is where files live. Bundling them is a convenience, not a technical requirement. If the domain is at Namecheap and the site is on GitHub Pages or another host, you will create DNS records at Namecheap pointing at the host. That is normal.

Namecheap also sells hosting (see their official site). Hostinger sells hosting and also sells domains. We are not quoting TLD prices, “free domain” promo rules, or hosting SKUs — those change. Check each provider’s current pricing page.

Criteria

  1. Do you even need hosting? Static HTML: Pages first. See the student sequence.
  2. Registrar quality vs host quality. Grade the DNS UI, lock, 2FA, and transfer process separately from PHP/MySQL. A good registrar is not automatically a good shared host.
  3. Nameservers. If hosting stays at Namecheap, they may ask you to use their nameservers. If hosting is elsewhere, you usually keep Namecheap DNS and add A/CNAME/AAAA records. Read the official docs for the layout you pick.
  4. Email. Domain + hosting bundles sometimes include mail, sometimes not. Check the live plan matrix. School email may already cover you.
  5. Panel and app install. Same beginner questions as best hosting for beginners.
  6. Export and transfer. Domain transfers and hosting file exports are different procedures. Confirm both before you put a grade-dependent site on a bundle.
  7. Renewals. Domain renewals and hosting renewals are separate invoices even when they share a cart. We will not invent a year-two price.

A split that is often rational

  • Keep the registrar you already understand (Namecheap is a common one) for the domain.
  • Keep static files on GitHub Pages until you need PHP.
  • If you need shared-style hosting, compare Hostinger with other shared-style hosts using criteria — including Hostinger vs SiteGround — rather than assuming the registrar’s hosting add-on is mandatory.

The reverse split (Hostinger domain + other hosting) is also valid. We are not paid to prefer one cart.
