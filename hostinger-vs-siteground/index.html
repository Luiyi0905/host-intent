Hostinger vs SiteGround: two beginner-shaped hosts, no scoreboard

Hostinger and SiteGround are both names you will see next to “shared hosting” and first WordPress installs. They compete for similar jobs more than Hostinger vs Cloudways does. We still refuse a numeric winner. This page is a criteria sheet.

Who this is for

You decided you need a conventional paid host (PHP, panel, app installer) after reading beginner criteria or student hosting. You are down to these two logos and want a way to choose without a fake 9.8 vs 9.6.

Who this is not for

Teams that already want managed cloud VMs — that is Hostinger vs Cloudways. Static-only sites — use GitHub Pages.

What we can say without inventing facts

Both companies publicly sell web hosting aimed at individuals and small organizations. Both publish knowledge bases. Both have affiliate programs we have not been approved for. Beyond that, plan names, included “free” extras, datacenter lists, and sticker prices change. Check each provider’s current pricing page and feature matrix.

  • Hostinger: https://www.hostinger.com/
  • SiteGround: https://www.siteground.com/

Criteria to apply on their live pages

  1. Plan term and renewal language. Introductory checkout prices are not the same as year-two prices. We will not quote either. Read both the cart and the renewal paragraph.
  2. Datacenter region vs your audience. If your classmates are in one country, a faraway region can add latency. We have not pinged their POPs from El Salvador or from a US metro. Pick from the list they show at signup.
  3. App and PHP version policy. If your assignment needs a specific PHP version, confirm it in the official docs, not a YouTube video.
  4. Staging / site copy. Useful for homework you must not break. Presence or absence belongs on the official feature list, not in our memory.
  5. Email hosting. Do not assume mailboxes. Check the plan table.
  6. Support surface. Chat vs tickets vs phone. We have not mystery-shopped wait times.
  7. Backup restore, not backup logo. Find the restore steps in the KB.
  8. Export. Files + database dump without a paid concierge.

Working method (do this in one sitting)

  1. Open both official homepages in two tabs.
  2. Write your actual need: static vs CMS, email vs not, expected months online.
  3. Fill the criteria list with quotes from their pages, dated in your notes.
  4. If the need was “a page for class,” stop and reconsider GitHub Pages.
